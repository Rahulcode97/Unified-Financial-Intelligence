How I lead

Four habits, shown in two flagship projects.

Researchmarks examples from the research intelligence platform. Financemarks examples from the finance suite.

Hiring

  • Set the technical bar and ran the rounds. Screened 20+ candidates and made the final call on 4 engineering hires for the data/AI function
  • Trained some of them as they started

Start from the people, not the code

  • ResearchInterviewed marketing, directors and juniors. Found the real problem was who to target, not how many to reach
  • FinanceBuilt around the finance team's month-end workflow, replacing a week of spreadsheet matching with one click
  • Measure success by adoption and outcomes: an 8% → 36% open rate, and a week of work cut to under an hour

Own it end to end

  • ResearchRun the Hostinger VPS and Coolify, the databases, access gateways, release pipelines and a 10-PC fleet
  • FinanceArchitected the stack from gateway APIs to a MySQL warehouse on Google Cloud
  • Security by default in both: invite-only sign-in, RBAC, encrypted secrets, audit logs
  • Cost discipline: repurposed idle hardware and used a local model before paying for the LLM

Make everyone else faster

  • ResearchA plain-English ops copilot let juniors and directors run the fleet themselves, with guardrails
  • ResearchAbout 30 hours a week of repetitive spreadsheet work removed from the junior team
  • FinanceA finance chatbot gives leadership answers and charts on demand, over web and Telegram

Principles

How I work.

One owner, end to end

Requirements, build, deploy and support all sit with me, so nothing falls between teams.

Cost-conscious by default

Reuse idle hardware, self-host where it pays, and automate only what's worth automating.

Security from day one

Role-based access, scoped keys, encrypted secrets and safe LLM tools are part of the first version.

Ship, then listen

Watch how people actually use it, then iterate. Adoption is the real measure.
