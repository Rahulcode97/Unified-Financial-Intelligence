How I lead

Four examples, each with a result.

Researchmarks the research intelligence platform. Financemarks the finance suite. Teammarks the people I hired.

Decide with the people who use it

  • ResearchSituation: marketing planned outreach from Excel lists, and only 8% of emails were opened
  • Action: interviewed marketing, directors and juniors before writing code, and found the real problem was who to target, not how many to reach. Built classification and work-history ranking around that
  • Result: open rate rose from 8% to 36% within two months of rollout

Build it so others can run it

  • ResearchSituation: a 10-PC collection fleet and the surrounding spreadsheets needed constant manual attention
  • Action: built a plain-English ops copilot with dry-run, audit log, undo and code-level safety bounds, so non-engineers can operate it safely
  • Result: about 30 hours a week of repetitive work removed, and the data operations team and directors use it daily

Keep engineers unblocked

  • TeamSituation: two full-stack engineers work on their own projects, separate from mine, and still need servers, deployments and keys
  • Action: I run the Hostinger VPS and Coolify, deployments, access gateways and key issuance for them, alongside my own six systems
  • Result: they stay on their own projects while infrastructure and access are handled centrally

Design around the real workflow

  • FinanceSituation: month-end meant about a week of matching CCAvenue, PayPal and bank transfers by hand
  • Action: built the suite around the finance team's own month-end steps, from gateway APIs to a MySQL warehouse on Google Cloud
  • Result: a week of work now takes under an hour, across ₹63 Cr processed over five years

Standards

What I hold across all six.

Security from day one

Invite-only sign-in, role-based access, scoped keys, encrypted secrets, audit logs and safe LLM tools are part of the first version.

Cost-conscious by default

Repurposed idle hardware, self-hosted where it pays, and a local model before paying for an LLM.

Ship, then listen

Watch how people actually use it, then iterate. Adoption is the real measure.
